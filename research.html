Orbital Debris and Collision Avoidance in Low Earth Orbit

Research study of orbital debris growth, collision risk modeling, and mitigation strategies for spacecraft operating in Low Earth Orbit.

Conducted a comprehensive study on orbital debris growth, tracking limitations, collision risk modeling, and debris mitigation strategies for spacecraft operating in Low Earth Orbit (LEO). Integrated NASA's DRAMA tool, SSA processes, conjunction assessment frameworks, and emerging technologies in autonomous avoidance.

  • LEO debris population is increasing exponentially due to fragmentation events and mega-constellations.
  • Collision risk grows significantly when spacecraft uncertainties overlap ("probability of collision" model).
  • Maneuver planning becomes fuel-limited and time-sensitive when conjunction windows are short.
  • Ground-based laser photon pressure can produce measurable delta-v (~1 cm/s) for debris displacement.
  • Used NASA's DRAMA framework to analyze conjunction frequency, atmospheric drag effects, and reentry survivability models.
  • Evaluated covariance-based probability of collision (Pc) using position and velocity uncertainties.
  • Compared threshold Pc values used by NASA and ESA for deciding automatic vs. manual avoidance maneuvers.
  • Analyzed conjunction tables showing severity categories based on kinetic energy and breakup potential.
  • Case-by-case maneuver planning using delta-v optimization and timeline constraints.
  • Autonomous avoidance using linear programming for fast, low-fuel trajectory adjustments.
  • Laser photon-pressure displacement for pushing small debris off collision paths.
  • ESA debris mitigation practices: passivation, 25-year deorbit rule, fragmentation control.

Magnetic Alignment Dynamics

Technical report on magnetic alignment dynamics for a drone payload docking system, developed as a numerical modeling study of connector alignment during payload capture.

Modeled a two-axis magnetic docking connector for a heavy-lift payload drone using nonlinear rotational differential equations. The study represents pitch and yaw alignment through a ball-joint-style connector model, where magnetic restoring torque pulls the connector toward alignment and damping reduces oscillation during docking.

  • Developed a two-axis rotational dynamics model for payload connector alignment.
  • Represented magnetic restoring torque as the primary alignment mechanism.
  • Included damping to evaluate settling behavior and reduce overshoot during docking.
  • Used MATLAB-based ODE simulation to study connector response over time.
  • Magnetic restoring torque can drive passive connector self-alignment in pitch and yaw.
  • Damping is important for reducing oscillatory motion before final payload capture.
  • The simplified dynamics model provides a foundation for future connector geometry and control studies.

Causal Analysis of Aviation Accident Lethality

Research project applying causal discovery and statistical modeling to historical fatal aviation accident data in order to study factors associated with high-lethality outcomes.

Analyzed 5,268 fatal aviation accidents from 1908-2009 to identify contributing factors associated with high-lethality outcomes. Converted unstructured crash summaries into structured cause categories using keyword classification, then applied causal discovery techniques to move beyond correlation-based safety analysis.

  • Used Python to structure accident records and classify narrative summaries into cause categories.
  • Applied FCI causal discovery with chi-square conditional independence testing.
  • Used domain-informed constraints to make the causal graph more realistic for aviation safety analysis.
  • Estimated causal effects using regression-based modeling after the graph discovery stage.
  • Evaluated how factors such as CFIT, weather, military operation status, fuel issues, and mechanical failure influenced accident severity.
  • Used causal graph structure to separate likely directional relationships from simple correlations.
  • Presented the work as a six-person team project combining data cleaning, causal inference, and regression analysis.
