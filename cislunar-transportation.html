Two-Semester Aerospace Design Project

Cislunar Transportation Architecture

In Progress Preliminary Design · Fall 2026–Spring 2027

Developing an integrated transportation architecture for sustained human and cargo operations throughout cislunar space.

Current phase: Preparing for the Systems Requirements Review.

The Design Challenge

Connecting Earth and cislunar space

The course brief challenges the team to design a transportation architecture for personnel and cargo serving at least three different types of cislunar habitation. Potential uses include tourism, research, logistics, and manufacturing. Each habitation needs justified locations, crew needs and turnover, and cargo mass, volume, and delivery frequency.

The scope includes launch, space transport, and landing vehicles, together with supporting infrastructure such as recovery facilities, propellant depots, and docking or transfer stations. Minimizing life-cycle cost, accounting for propellant availability, and considering vehicle reusability are central design objectives.

The project is in preliminary development, with assumptions and architecture choices still under review.

Current Engineering Work

Building the design foundation

Current work focuses on comparing transportation concepts, organizing the vehicle architecture, and translating mission needs into preliminary system requirements.

01 / Draft Trade Studies

Launch & In-Space Transportation

The team's draft matrices compare options for Earth-to-orbit launch and transportation from Earth orbit to the Moon. Evaluation criteria include safety, reusability, reliability, complexity, transportation time, payload capacity, and efficiency.

02 / Initial Work Breakdown

Vehicle & Subsystem Organization

An initial three-stage concept organizes payload, landing, and propulsion functions. The work breakdown identifies supporting elements such as guidance and control, propellant storage, life support, and heat shielding.

03 / Requirements Development

System Requirements Outline

The draft requirements table follows the numbered work breakdown structure, starting with a mission-level goal of safe, timely transportation. Detailed subsystem requirements remain to be developed as the concept matures.
